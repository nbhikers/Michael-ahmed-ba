FCA Consumer Duty — The Four Consumer Outcomes

The FCA requires firms to demonstrate good outcomes across four specific areas. Below is a precise mapping of how my process architecture work at Royal London evidenced compliance against each outcome.

Outcome 1

Products & Services

FCA requirement: Products and services must be fit for purpose and designed to meet the needs of the target market.
  • Delivered 19 end-to-end BPMN 2.0 Level 4 process maps within the Update Plan Details L3 process — covering Add/Update Beneficiary, Change Plan Cover, Cancellations, Change of Policy Ownership, Add/Update Trust and 14 further processes — providing documented evidence that each product instruction is executed as designed
  • As-Is / To-Be process mapping across all 19 Level 4 processes identified where operational delivery diverged from product design — creating a structured gap register for remediation
  • Process architecture established a baseline against which product changes could be assessed for operational impact, ensuring new products remain operationally viable before launch
  • Defined process ownership at named individual level, creating clear accountability for ongoing product delivery standards
Outcome 2

Price & Value

FCA requirement: Firms must ensure the price consumers pay is reasonable relative to the overall benefits of the product or service.
  • SOP rationalisation (60–70% volume reduction across ~57 documents within Update Plan Details) eliminated duplicate, contradictory and inefficient procedures — directly reducing the operational overhead embedded in product pricing
  • Process efficiency improvements identified through To-Be mapping surfaced opportunities to reduce unit costs in customer operations, supporting fair value assessments
  • RPA and AI automation opportunities identified within process landscape and costed via business cases — enabling the Group to demonstrate a programme of ongoing value improvement to regulators
  • Standardised process documentation created the operational transparency needed to evidence that customer charges reflect genuine operational costs, not inefficiency
Outcome 3

Consumer Understanding

FCA requirement: Customer communications must be clear, timely and help consumers make informed decisions.
  • Removal of contradictory guidance across fragmented SOP estates eliminated the risk of agents providing inconsistent or confusing information to customers at key touchpoints
  • Defined 'definition of done' at each process stage — including customer communication steps — ensuring agents know exactly what information must be provided, in what format, and when
  • Workshop findings registers captured where process steps created ambiguity in customer-facing communications, driving targeted remediation
  • Process maps made decision gateways explicit — ensuring complex scenarios (e.g. Group Pensions, Personal Pensions, policy amendments) follow documented pathways with clear customer notification requirements at each branch
  • Standardised processes across cross-functional teams eliminated inconsistency in customer-facing communications at every Update Plan Details touchpoint
Outcome 4

Consumer Support

FCA requirement: Firms must provide support that meets consumers' needs throughout the product lifecycle.
  • Standardised end-to-end process maps ensure every customer receives consistent service quality regardless of which team member handles their case — eliminating postcode lottery service delivery
  • Named process ownership and defined review cadences created a continuous improvement pipeline — ensuring support standards evolve as customer needs change, not stagnate
  • Release and readiness packs documented all process changes with clear handover criteria — ensuring frontline teams are equipped to deliver improved support before changes go live
  • Governance structures (findings register, decision log, change log with full audit trail) provide the regulator with auditable evidence that support processes are actively managed and improved
  • Collaboration with risk, compliance and audit teams embedded regulatory oversight directly into process governance — not as a retrospective check, but as a live control

User Story: Update Plan Details — Royal London Group

A worked example of how I translate stakeholder needs into structured, governance-ready requirements — with clear acceptance criteria and traceability throughout.

User Story · Life & Pensions · Customer Operations · Update Plan Details

Process Taxonomy Context

Level 1: Customer Account Management  →  Level 2: Execute Customer Instruction  →  Level 3: Update Plan Details (19 Level 4 processes · 57 Level 5 processes)

Context

During the Group-wide process architecture programme, workshop sessions with cross-functional teams revealed that service teams were relying on 57 fragmented SOPs across the Update Plan Details L3 process — with no consistent entry point, contradictory guidance within documents, and no defined 'definition of done'. This created inconsistent customer outcomes and an unauditable process trail.

"As a Customer Operations Specialist at Royal London, I need a single, clearly structured end-to-end process guide for updating plan details — so that I can action every customer instruction consistently, meet our Consumer Duty obligations, and provide auditors with a clear evidence trail of customer outcomes."

Acceptance Criteria

  • 19 BPMN 2.0 Level 4 process maps exist covering the complete Update Plan Details lifecycle — from instruction received through to confirmed update and closure
  • Each process map has a named owner, version number, effective date and scheduled review date
  • All 57 legacy SOPs have been reviewed; contradictory guidance resolved; consolidated into a single supporting document per Level 5 process
  • Each process step has a defined 'definition of done' and a clear decision gateway for exceptions
  • Each process map has been reviewed and signed off by Level 3 & Level 4 Owners, Risk & Compliance, and the Group Process Owner
  • A findings register records all issues identified, decisions taken, and rationale — providing a full audit trail
  • The new processes are published in the approved document management system and superseded SOPs are archived with a redirect notice

Regulatory Mapping (Consumer Duty)

  • Consumer Support: Standardised processes ensure every customer instruction is handled consistently regardless of which team member manages the case
  • Consumer Understanding: Removal of contradictory SOP guidance eliminates the risk of agents providing inconsistent or confusing communications to customers
  • Products & Services: End-to-end process documentation evidences that plan update instructions are executed as designed and regulatory expectations are met
  • Price & Value: SOP rationalisation (57 → consolidated guides) reduces operational overhead, supporting fair value in the cost of service delivery

Deliverables Produced

  • 19 BPMN 2.0 Level 4 process maps (As-Is and To-Be versions) covering all Update Plan Details processes
  • SOP rationalisation: 57 documents → 1 consolidated guide per Level 5 process
  • Definition of done for each process stage
  • Findings register with full decision log and rationale
  • Stakeholder sign-off documentation and release pack (signed by L3 & L4 Owners, Risk & Compliance, Group Process Owner)
  • Change log: what changed, why, when, who approved
